How to judge — the four buttons, with one example each
Accept (a)
Anything a colleague could act on, or you could answer a question from, as written. Nothing needs fixing. Example: “I work at Acme on the payments team, and I moved to Berlin in March.” It is complete, it has a subject, and the wording is what you would use yourself.
Accept with edits (e)
The content is worth keeping but the wording is not shippable: filler at the front, two facts welded together, a pronoun with no referent, a fragment with no verb. You type the version you would keep, and pressing Enter records it as an accept whose text is yours. Example: “By the way, I've been really enjoying editing my photos in Lightroom - the online communities have been super helpful.” → you would type “I edit photos in Lightroom and I am active in online photo-editing communities.”
Dismiss (d)
Nothing worth keeping, or nothing a colleague could use: a reaction, a pleasantry, a request you made of your assistant, a restatement of what the assistant just said, or something so vague that it carries no information. Example: “Thank you, that's really helpful!” Example: “Can you write the texts for the presentation ?” — that is a task you gave the assistant, not a fact about you.
Skip (s)
You genuinely cannot judge: the fragment has no context, or it is the same statement as an earlier card and you will not count it twice. Skip costs the pair — a pair is only scored when both of its presentations were judged, so skipping removes one from N and the report says so. Prefer a real decision when the hesitation is only “is this interesting?”.

Every card comes back a second time in the other presentation. Judge each showing on its own: do not try to remember what you said about the first one, and do not try to guess which side was written by the model. The page does not say which is which, but you will notice (one is a paragraph, one is a list) — that is a known limit of this measurement. Use the same standard on both.

Nothing here is published and nothing is scored until you run the command on the last screen. Every judgement is saved to this browser the moment you make it, so closing the tab loses nothing.
